# Frag5 rendering, affine and radiometry: primary-source contract audit

22 November 2026. Metadata and source code only. No CT chunk, TIFF intensity, PNG pixel, photograph payload, model output or PSD was acquired or decoded for this audit. Direct code/metadata response bodies totaled **3,114,259 bytes**, below the 16 MiB cap; the separate header/geometry agent owns its own range ledger. This is a contract report, not an experimental result or an authorization to acquire pixels. **From a source** means an inspected public document/code/metadata record; **My inference** identifies interpretation or a proposed check. **I do not know** is deliberate where provenance is incomplete.

## 2. PPM semantics and source identity

**From source code.** A PPM is an ordered raster of six doubles per pixel: source-volume **X,Y,Z** followed by **normal-X,normal-Y,normal-Z**. Its UV row/column indexing is `PPM[y,x]`. The generator interpolates mesh positions and unit normals at the UV position; its flat-shading alternative normalizes the triangle cross-product. The binary writer serializes native double memory in row-major order. Its header has width, height, dimensionality, ordered flag, type and format version, but **no declared byte order, coordinate units, physical ink face or source-volume UUID**. [PPM generator](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/texturing/src/PPMGenerator.cpp), [normal interpolation](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/BarycentricCoordinates.cpp), [PPM I/O](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/PerPixelMap.cpp), [ordered binary I/O](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/include/vc/core/io/PointSetIO.hpp).

**From the separate header/geometry execution record.** Frag5 has width **4560**, height **7309**, data offset **73 bytes**, and **48-byte records**. Thus record `(y,x)` begins at `73 + 48*(4560*y+x)`. The declared 85-record geometric lattice yielded 21 all-zero records and 64 nonzero records; the latter are compatible with little-endian doubles and unit normals. This is file-specific empirical endian evidence, not a universal PPM-format guarantee. All 64 source centers and their affine images fit the candidate fine/coarse bounds. The header report is `frag5-preflight/header_results.json`; its geometric evidence is `frag5-preflight/geometry_results.json`. These observations do not authenticate source identity by themselves.

**My inference.** The official fine-scan directory, coordinate extents and volume metadata support using fine UUID `20231121133215` as the candidate source. They do not prove it. The official [renders directory](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/renders/) contains only a placeholder; no invocation or render graph was found there. A fixed numerical TIFF-versus-raw-fine comparison is an appropriate next identity check. Do not call the PPM's normal positive direction “ink-facing” or “recto” without independent evidence.

## 3. Exact reference renderer and the unresolved phase

**From source code.** In VC 2.26.0, the line sampler uses the stored normal directly; it does not renormalize it during sampling. For bidirectional radius `r` and interval `h`, output position `k` samples

`q(k) = p + (-abs(r) + k*h) * n`,

with `floor(2*abs(r)/h)+1` samples. Samples run from negative to positive stored-normal direction. The layer renderer copies them to uint16 output planes, and the sequence writer enumerates from zero with decimal padding. There is no layer-order reversal or per-plane intensity rescaling on this TIFF path. [LineGenerator](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/LineGenerator.cpp), [LayerTexture](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/texturing/src/LayerTexture.cpp), [sequence writer](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/include/vc/core/io/ImageIO.hpp), [image writer](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/ImageIO.cpp).

**From source code.** Raw-volume interpolation treats integer XYZ coordinates as voxel centers, reads values as `slice[z][y,x]`, combines all eight neighbors in double precision, then applies **`cvRound` and casts to uint16**. There is no extra half-voxel displacement. Its historical implementation returns zero for an out-of-bounds center or corner; a new audit should reject incomplete interpolation neighborhoods instead of imitating boundary padding. The exact OpenCV build, floating-point environment and tie behavior of the original executable are not recorded by the TIFF version field. [Volume interpolation](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/Volume.cpp).

**From sources / important qualification.** The EduceLab-Scrolls paper specifies **r=32, h=1**, producing 65 planes for its Paris-fragment experiment. Under that recipe, TIFFs **00,32,64** are `p-32*n`, `p`, `p+32*n`. However, the VC utility's actual default radius is **material thickness/(2×voxel pitch)**, not 32. Current Frag5 `config.json` has thickness **150 µm**; with 3.24 µm pitch, the default radius is about **23.1481 voxels**, giving **47** samples at the default interval of one. Therefore 65 filenames alone do not prove Frag5's radius, interval or bidirectional mode. [Paper §A.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02084v4#A1.SS6), [exact utility](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/apps/src/LayerFromPPM.cpp), [Frag5 config](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/config.json).

**From source code.** The utility's `--negative-normal` flag controls only an optional newly generated flattened PPM. It does not flip the input PPM normals or reverse the TIFF sequence. The new flattened PPM uses XYZ=`(x,y,(plane_count-1)/2)` and normal `(0,0,±1)`. [Exact utility](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/apps/src/LayerFromPPM.cpp).

**My inference / prospective test.** Freeze the 32/1/bidirectional recipe as the candidate identity contract and compare a few geometry-selected points across all declared depths against raw fine CT. Preserve the stored normal, double arithmetic, center convention and rounding. State any one-count rounding tolerance before acquisition. A mismatch is a contract failure to diagnose separately; do not silently fit phase, normal sign, coordinates or intensity scale to maximize agreement. Even a successful limited check will establish consistency at those checked rays, not global source identity or coarse registration accuracy.

## 4. Affine convention and two meanings of a transformed normal

**From source metadata/code.** The [published transform](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/transforms/20231121133215-to-20231130111236.json) declares the fine UUID as `source` and coarse UUID as `target`. The reader copies the JSON 4×4 `params` matrix directly. It multiplies the column `[X,Y,Z,1]` for points and `[NX,NY,NZ,0]` for vectors. The intended volume-coordinate mapping is therefore **fine XYZ voxel indices → coarse XYZ voxel indices**; conversion to NumPy indexing is separately XYZ→ZYX. The matrix already contains the fitted scaling, rotation/shear and translation. Do not replace its linear component by the nominal pitch ratio or reapply the reconstruction crop rotation. [Transform implementation](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/Transforms.cpp), [transform interface](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/include/vc/core/types/Transforms.hpp).

**From source code.** Literal VC `ApplyTransform(PPM)` uses **normalized `A*n`** by default. It does **not** use inverse transpose for the stored normal. [Implementation](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/Transforms.cpp).

**My inference / mathematics.** Preserving a source ray maps `p+d*n` to `A*p+b+d*A*n`. Reconstructing the geometric normal to an affine-transformed surface instead uses `A^{-T}*n`, normalized in the relevant coordinate metric. These are distinct procedures under anisotropic scale/shear. A new protocol can choose either explicitly, but should not describe inverse-transpose rendering as a literal reproduction of the historical VC normal-transform code. The already published five-landmark sensitivity audit remains separate; no landmark calculation was repeated here.

## 5. Radiometry: known windows, unresolved Hecate conversion

**From a source.** The 2023 acquisition information sheet states that reconstruction produced float32 merged volumes, then full-volume **0.01 and 99.99 percentiles** were used to window them to 0–65535 uint16. It describes FBP and stitched grid scans, while leaving further reconstruction-filter details forthcoming. It also warns that stated energies are nominal and may differ from actual energies by a few keV. Thus “same nominal 70 keV” is the supported description. [Information sheet §§4–6](https://dl.ash2txt.org/other/EduceLab%20Herculaneum%20Scroll%20Data%20%282023%29%20Info%20Sheet%20v3.pdf).

**From exact source metadata.** The two published windows differ:

| Scan | Float reconstruction minimum | Float reconstruction maximum | Recorded uint16 value of reconstructed zero |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Fine 3.24 µm | -6.489617234328761e-05 | 0.00014518542961595255 | 20244 |
| Native coarse 7.91 µm | -5.061501632080763e-05 | 0.00019497037940743444 | 13506 |

[Fine window](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/working/PHerc1667Cr01Fr03_70keV_3.24um/metadata/window.json), [coarse window](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/working/PHerc1667Cr01Fr03_70keV_7.91um/metadata/window.json).

**My inference.** Equal uint16 values across these acquisitions are not equal reconstruction-domain values. A declared approximate inverse-window mapping can remove the known affine display window away from clipping, but the window metadata does not certify a common calibrated attenuation unit, matched PSF/noise, or identical reconstruction filtering. No per-test-patch min/max fitting should be disguised as a published conversion.

**From source code.** Hecate's pinned release requires **unnormalized uint8 ZYX**. It divides coarse 9.6 µm inputs by **255** and fine 2.4 µm inputs by **200**; it contains no uint16 input converter. The model-interface divisor does not identify how Frag5's uint16 source should become uint8. [Hecate at 9cb86e5](https://huggingface.co/scrollprize/hecate/blob/9cb86e500e944b11a06a7020403cde5dffb5bcb2/hecate.py).

**From source code.** Historical VC's layer-TIFF path preserves uint16. Its generic optional `QuantizeImage` can either rescale observed min/max or the full dtype range, depending on an argument. The current Villa renderer also preserves uint16 source bit depth; a separate TIFF converter has an optional uint16→uint8 factor **1/257**. These are distinct tools/options, not evidence that any one created Hecate's training inputs. [Historical conversion helper](https://github.com/educelab/volume-cartographer/blob/7640ba6c8e41f39a7aef63e9780cd13c296b2d15/core/src/ImageConversion.cpp), [current renderer](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/blob/c8f3ba45304f0a317a49a155929e30d294f201eb/volume-cartographer/apps/src/vc_render_tifxyz.cpp), [current TIFF converter](https://github.com/ScrollPrize/villa/blob/c8f3ba45304f0a317a49a155929e30d294f201eb/vc_object_store/src/Tiff.cpp).

**From metadata / unresolved.** Official separate `volumes_standardized` fine and coarse Zarrs exist with matching grid shapes and **uint8** dtype. Their inspected attributes declare relative pyramid scales but no standardization recipe. No chunks were read. **I do not know** their exact conversion or whether it matches Hecate training. They are distinct potential sources, not permission to substitute them into a raw-uint16 protocol. [Fine standardized metadata](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/volumes_standardized/70keV_3.24um_.zarr/0/.zarray), [coarse standardized metadata](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/volumes_standardized/70keV_7.91um_.zarr/0/.zarray), [attributes](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/volumes_standardized/70keV_7.91um_.zarr/.zattrs).

## 6. Naples label provenance is still incomplete

**From sources.** The original EduceLab-Scrolls paper documents manual photograph-to-CT deformable registration and ink tracing under papyrologist supervision for its four Paris fragments. Its detailed demonstration does not identify the current Naples Frag5 PNG or PSD as an output of that exact process. The official Frag5 directory includes IR, ink-label PNG and editable PSD files. These facts support an intended photographic reference but do not authenticate the annotation history of the exact current binary asset. [Paper §§2.1,A.8–A.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02084v4), [official bundle](https://dl.ash2txt.org/fragments/Frag5/PHerc1667Cr1Fr3.volpkg/working/PHerc1667Cr01Fr03_70keV_3.24um/surface_processing/).

**From a source.** An official 30 October 2024 announcement describes Youssef Nader's project on segmentation, ink detection and **iterative labeling specifically for Fragment 5**. It does not identify the current `inklabels.png` or establish that those labels were replaced by his project. The relevant project link is a Discord discussion; no contact or messaging occurred, and no sign-in route was used. [Official announcement](https://scrollprize.substack.com/p/exciting-acceleration-in-community).

**I do not know** who traced/approved the exact current Naples label, whether every stroke was derived exclusively from the IR photograph, the exact warp history or its residuals, or whether a model ever assisted its production. The presence of the IR file and a PSD, or matching dimensions, is not sufficient to assert independence from model outputs. The separate IHDR audit found the labels to be a one-bit palette PNG; palette polarity is also not established from IHDR alone. A future explicitly authorized metadata-only PLTE/tRNS inspection can settle palette encoding, but not authorship or annotation independence.
